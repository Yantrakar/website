What teams want AI on

Teams want AI on their business intelligence, source code, meeting notes, finance data, project plans and draft quotations. Stopping AI use is not an option anymore. When the data has to stay in-house, the models can run inside your own network. People also call this local AI, self-hosted AI or on-premise AI: open-weight models, including large language models (LLMs), on servers you control.

A model chosen for each job

So far I see that the largest frontier models are not always needed. What helps is a harness, a small layer that routes each request, searches your documents and checks access, plus an open-weight model that is good enough for each job. My first attempt in 2024 failed because open-weight models weren't ready. They are now good enough for many everyday jobs, and lower running costs made it practical to use hardware a company already has.

How a delivery goes

Setting it up

  1. Use-case study. Which work, which data, who uses it, how often.
  2. Model choice. An open-weight model per use case, tested on your own examples.
  3. Deploy on your hardware. Automated with Ansible, so it can be rebuilt and moved.
  4. Index your code and documents. A local indexer, so answers come from your material.

After that, ongoing

Monitor and upgrade. Open-weight models improve quickly. Swapping one in is planned for from the start.

Before you start

Tuning is ongoing. In a test in October 2026, I made 120 mixed runs with long inputs, from 100,000 to 250,000 tokens. A cache kept building up and bloated the system. I found a way to recover from it, and I am now looking for a way to avoid it.

Everyday interactive use needs capable hardware, so I would not judge the idea on a laptop test. Some work runs well on modest hardware, such as the two first use cases below.
